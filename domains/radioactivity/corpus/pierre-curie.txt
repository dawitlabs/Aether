Pierre Curie was a French physicist, born in Paris in 1859. With his brother Jacques he discovered piezoelectricity in 1880, the electric charge that appears in certain crystals when they are squeezed. He also studied magnetism, and the temperature at which a material loses its permanent magnetism is called the Curie point after him.

He married Marie Skłodowska in 1895 and joined her research on radioactivity. Together they discovered polonium and radium in 1898, and in 1903 they shared the Nobel Prize in Physics with Henri Becquerel. Pierre Curie died in Paris in 1906 after being struck by a horse-drawn wagon.
