Radioactive decay is the process in which an unstable atomic nucleus releases energy by emitting radiation. The three classic kinds of radiation are alpha, beta, and gamma. Alpha particles are helium nuclei, beta particles are fast electrons, and gamma rays are high-energy electromagnetic radiation.

When a nucleus gives off an alpha or beta particle it becomes the nucleus of a different element. The moment at which a single nucleus decays cannot be predicted, but a large number of identical nuclei decay at a steady average rate. This rate is described by the half-life: the time it takes for half of the nuclei in a sample to decay.
