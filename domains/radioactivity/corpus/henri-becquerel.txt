Henri Becquerel was a French physicist, born in Paris in 1852 into a family of scientists. In 1896 he found that uranium salts left an image on photographic plates kept in the dark, even when no sunlight had reached the salts. He concluded that uranium gave off penetrating rays of its own, and this observation is counted as the discovery of radioactivity.

For this work Becquerel shared the 1903 Nobel Prize in Physics with Pierre Curie and Marie Curie, who had carried his discovery much further. He died in 1908.
