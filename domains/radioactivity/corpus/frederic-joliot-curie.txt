Frédéric Joliot-Curie was a French physicist and chemist, born in 1900. He joined the Radium Institute in Paris as an assistant to Marie Curie, where he met her daughter Irène; they married in 1926 and both took the name Joliot-Curie.

Together they discovered artificial radioactivity in 1934 by bombarding aluminium with alpha particles, and they received the 1935 Nobel Prize in Chemistry for it. He later took a leading role in French nuclear research. He died in 1958.
