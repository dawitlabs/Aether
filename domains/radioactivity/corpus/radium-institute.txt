The Radium Institute in Paris was founded in 1909 as a joint project of the University of Paris and the Pasteur Institute, and its buildings were finished in 1914. It had two parts: a laboratory for physics and chemistry led by Marie Curie, and a laboratory for the biological and medical study of radiation led by the physician Claudius Regaud.

The institute became a centre for research on radioactivity and for the use of radiation to treat cancer. Irène and Frédéric Joliot-Curie made their discovery of artificial radioactivity there in 1934. The institute is one of the origins of today's Institut Curie.
