Radium is a chemical element with the symbol Ra and atomic number 88. All of its isotopes are radioactive; the longest-lived, radium-226, has a half-life of about 1,600 years. Pure radium is a soft, silvery metal, and its radiation can make certain materials glow, which is why it was once used in luminous paint for watch dials.

Marie and Pierre Curie announced the discovery of radium in 1898 after processing large amounts of uranium ore. In 1910 Marie Curie and André-Louis Debierne isolated radium as a pure metal for the first time. Radium is dangerous to handle because its radiation damages living tissue and because the body stores it in bones in place of calcium.
