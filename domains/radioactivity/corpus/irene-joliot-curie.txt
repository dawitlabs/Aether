Irène Joliot-Curie, born in Paris in 1897, was the elder daughter of Marie and Pierre Curie. As a young woman she helped her mother run X-ray units during the First World War, and she later worked at the Radium Institute in Paris.

In 1926 she married Frédéric Joliot, who also worked at the institute. In 1934 the couple showed that bombarding light elements such as aluminium with alpha particles produced new, radioactive atoms. This discovery of artificial radioactivity, also called induced radioactivity, earned them the 1935 Nobel Prize in Chemistry. Marie Curie and Irène Joliot-Curie are the only mother and daughter who have both won Nobel Prizes.

Irène Joliot-Curie died in 1956 of leukaemia, an illness linked to her long exposure to radiation.
